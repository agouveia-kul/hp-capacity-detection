## Explicit answers

1. **Can the 03b learning curve be extended beyond n = 62 train HP households?** **Yes, on GB-EoH 2021/22**: about 309 train HP households per seed are usable (stations with ≥ 3 in the split; the largest groups hold 102, 78 and 29), so n = 100, 200 and 300 are feasible. GB-RHPP reaches n = 86 and B\*+ does not go beyond B\*. Caveat: the curve would then test the data-limit diagnosis *within GB-EoH* (different climate, 30-min, fill from LCL 2012/13), not within B\*. A flat curve from 62 to 300 on GB-EoH would still be strong evidence that "more households" is not the lever.
2. **Does any new pool give ≥ 20 test substations per seed in every Paper A bin?** **Not with the v1 grid as it stands.** GB-EoH 2021/22 gives 265 / 145 / 30 / **15**, GB-RHPP 50 / 35 / 15 / 10 and NEEA 20 / 20 / 5 / 0. The > 65 % bin is limited by the grid (only p = 1.0 lies above 0.65), not by households. On GB-EoH, adding p = 0.8 or raising the test substations per cell from 5 to 7 reaches ≥ 20 in every bin (grid change → REVIEW reason).
3. **Should a new pool replace B\* as the headline?** **No; run GB-EoH as a second full-grid pool.** B\* keeps continuity with Paper A (Kloten, same households, same climate), has real same-period fill, and is the population the identifiability results were derived on. GB-EoH has no whole-house load, so its fill is 8–10 years older and from London, with s₀ 2.4× B\*'s. With the property table it now has a nameplate (`HP_Size_kW`), design heat load, type (incl. HT-ASHP) and postcode district for every home. It is the right pool for the N question (learning curve), for the top penetration bins, for the nameplate target and as the transfer domain. Revisit only if a concurrent GB whole-house source is obtained: SERL household data (UKDS SN 8666, Secure Access: analysis only inside SecureLab; the safeguarded SN 8963 is aggregated and cannot serve as fill; Agrawal et al. 2026 combine SERL with EoH).
