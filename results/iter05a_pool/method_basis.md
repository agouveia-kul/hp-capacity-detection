# 05a — Methodological basis of the analog-day filler (for the methods section)

**Assumption (conditional independence).** EoH records only the heat-pump system of each home, so a GB-EoH substation combines two datasets that were never observed jointly: EoH heat-pump electricity H (2021–23, homes grouped by weather station in Scotland, NE and SE England) and Low Carbon London household load L (flat-rate households, 2012–14, London). Composing net(t) = Σ L_i(d′, slot) + Σ H_j(t) assumes that, given X = (daily mean outdoor temperature, day type, time of year), the filler load is independent of the heat-pump load and of anything else about the target day. This is the conditional independence assumption of statistical matching [1]; it cannot be tested from the two datasets alone, which is why 05a bounds its error with two auxiliary checks (D4: LCL rebuilt from LCL; D5: B\* rebuilt from B\*, where the joint data exist).

**How the mapping implements it.** For every weather group g and day d, one LCL day d′ is drawn from the same day type (England & Wales calendar), within ±30 days of day-of-year (widened to 45, then 60 days only when no candidate is within 1 K), with |T_London(d′) − T_g(d)| ≤ 1 K, at random among the 3 closest in temperature (seeded, fixed per group and split seed); the same d′ serves every filler of the substation-day, and local clock time maps onto local clock time. This is k-nearest-neighbour resampling of whole days on a conditioning state inside a moving seasonal window, as in nonparametric weather generators [2, 3], and the analog method of statistical downscaling [4]; in the load domain it is the similar-day principle of pattern-similarity load forecasting [5] and of day-matching customer baselines for demand response [6]. London temperature is the HadCET daily series [7] (a Central England composite, not a London station).

**Energy-domain precedent.** Adding measured heat-pump profiles to separately measured demand has precedent at national scale: Love et al. [8] aggregate ~700 RHPP heat pumps and add the profile to GB grid demand. The closest household/LV precedent found is Agrawal & Wallom [9], who integrate SERL household smart-meter data with EoH heat-pump data into synthetic half-hourly household profiles, segmented by housing type and season, and validate them against an Oxford substation (from the abstract; their pairing rule was not checked in the full text). **No precedent was found for day-level, temperature-analog pairing of separately measured heat-pump and household loads from different years**; 05a's rule is our construction, justified by [1–6] and tested by D1–D5.

**What the mapping cannot recover.** (i) Within-household correlation: an HP home's own non-HP load (occupancy, cooking, DHW habits) is replaced by an unrelated household (D5 measures the cost on B\*). (ii) Cross-year drift: 2012–14 appliance stock, lighting and pre-COVID occupancy vs 2021–23. (iii) Weather other than daily mean T (solar gain and daylight beyond the ±30-day window, wind, humidity) and the gap between HadCET and the EoH stations' local climate. (iv) Day-to-day persistence: consecutive target days draw unrelated source days, so multi-day cold-spell dynamics and the filler's autocorrelation are not preserved. (v) The filler's own electric-heating response: all flat-rate households are used, so electrically heated LCL homes contribute to s₀ (a survey-based gas-only sensitivity subset is available, `lcl_audit.md`).
